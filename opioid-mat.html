Opioid MAT — ED-Initiated Buprenorphine

Medication for opioid use disorder (OUD) · CA Bridge induction · naloxone · referral · how to talk to the patient

Why This Matters
MAT Act 2023 · No X-waiver

ED-initiated buprenorphine roughly doubles engagement in addiction treatment at 30 days and reduces overdose death. Since the X-waiver was eliminated (MAT Act, Consolidated Appropriations Act 2023), any clinician with a standard DEA registration (Schedule III) may prescribe buprenorphine for OUD — no special waiver, no patient cap. California's CA Bridge model (buprenorphine + a substance-use navigator + take-home naloxone) is the statewide standard this page summarizes.

⚠️ Decision-support summary of the CA Bridge protocol — verify against current CA Bridge guidance and your KP SBC ED / Addiction Medicine protocol before use. Not a substitute for clinical judgment, pharmacy verification, or specialist consultation.

💉 Buprenorphine Induction (CA Bridge)
⚠️ Precipitated withdrawal risk. For observed/standard induction, wait until the patient is in objective opioid withdrawal — COWS ≥ 8 — before the first dose. High potency fentanyl complicates timing; consider low-dose ("micro") induction or specialist input when standard timing is difficult.

Standard (observed)Moderate withdrawal, COWS ≥ 8Start 8 mg SL; reassess ~30–60 min; repeat to relieve withdrawal. Target ~8 mg day 1, up to 16 mg (some protocols to 32 mg).
Start low, go slowBorderline COWS 8–9 or apprehensive2 mg SL, then 2–4 mg every 1–2 h to effect.
High-doseSevere withdrawal / high tolerance (fentanyl)Larger initial dose (e.g., 16 mg) with reassessment — per CA Bridge high-dose guidance.
Low-dose ("micro")Can't tolerate withdrawal, on methadone, or unclear last useVery small overlapping doses (e.g., start 0.5 mg) titrated up over days while full agonist continues — typically protocolized/specialist-guided.
After naloxone / overdosePost-reversal, once withdrawal emergesTreat emergent withdrawal with buprenorphine per CA Bridge post-overdose pathway.
Home / unobservedMild withdrawal or uncertain in EDGive the unobserved induction handout + 3–7 day Rx bup/naloxone SL to start at home when in withdrawal.
📝 Discharge & Prescribing
  • No X-waiver needed — prescribe buprenorphine/naloxone SL to bridge to the first clinic visit (e.g., a few days to ~1–2 weeks' supply per protocol).
  • Warm handoff: connect to a substance-use navigator / bridge clinic and addiction medicine before discharge (see referral lines below).
  • Take-home naloxone for every OUD patient and their household — see harm reduction.
  • Document OUD, withdrawal (COWS), shared decision-making, and follow-up plan.
🧯 Naloxone & Harm Reduction
  • Take-home naloxone (intranasal 4 mg): prescribe/dispense and teach the patient + family how to use it; furnishable without an individual Rx under California's standing order.
  • Overdose education: don't use alone, go slow with new supply, fentanyl is in most of the supply.
  • Fentanyl/xylazine test strips and safer-use supplies where available.
  • Meet patients where they are — offer naloxone and follow-up even if they decline medication today.
💬 How to Talk to the Patient

Brief, non-judgmental, and hopeful. Use motivational interviewing and person-first language ("person with opioid use disorder," not "addict"; "positive/negative" not "clean/dirty").

"We have a medication called buprenorphine that treats opioid use disorder. It eases withdrawal and cravings, and it cuts the risk of overdose death. We can start it today, right here."
"This is a medical condition, not a moral failing — and it's treatable. What matters to you about your health right now?"
"Even if you're not ready to stop today, I'd like to give you naloxone and connect you with someone who can help whenever you are."
  • Ask permission to discuss; affirm any step toward safety.
  • Normalize relapse as part of a chronic condition; avoid ultimatums.
  • Emphasize "you can start today" — same-visit initiation is a key driver of follow-up.
