ED Quick Summary
🧠

Acute ICH — First Moves in the ED

  • Initiate the ICH ED order set in Health Connect.
  • Target SBP 130–150 mmHg in the ED (avoid dropping <130 in mild–moderate ICH — see below).
  • Reverse oral anticoagulation rapidly — stop the agent and give the right reversal as soon as possible.
  • Do NOT give levetiracetam (Keppra) to all ICH patients — only for confirmed/suspected seizure.
  • Call early per the Neuro Hub pathway for vascular neurology / neurosurgery input.
Decision-support only. Adapted from the 2026 ICH Update — SBC ED Education (N. Sangha, MD, KP SCAL) and the 2022 AHA/ASA Guideline for the Management of Patients With Spontaneous Intracerebral Hemorrhage (Greenberg et al., Stroke 2022). Parenthetical tags are the guideline Class of Recommendation (COR). Always follow the current order set, pharmacy verification, and neuro/neurosurgery guidance.
Blood Pressure Management
🩸 Lowering BP in Acute ICH
  • Mild–moderate ICH presenting with SBP 150–220 → acutely lower to a target of 140, maintaining 130–150. COR 2b
  • Start treatment within 2 h of onset and reach target within 1 h to reduce hematoma expansion. COR 2a
  • Titrate for smooth, sustained control — avoid peaks and large SBP variability. COR 2a
  • Large/severe ICH or those needing surgical decompression → safety/efficacy of intensive lowering is not well established. COR 2b
  • If mild–moderate ICH presenting with SBP >150, acutely lowering to <130 is potentially harmful. 3: Harm
Goal in the ED: SBP 130–150. Use a titratable IV agent (e.g., nicardipine or clevidipine infusion) for smooth control.
Anticoagulant Reversal
Discontinue anticoagulation immediately. Rapid reversal should be performed as soon as possible. COR 1  Take a history of when the last dose was taken. For DOACs, give activated charcoal if last dose <2 h (potential efficacy up to 8 h). COR 2b

Antiplatelet-Related Hemorrhage
🧫 Aspirin / P2Y12 (clopidogrel, ticagrelor)

✓ Not having neurosurgery

Hold antiplatelet agents and DO NOT order a platelet transfusion. Platelet transfusion is associated with harm (PATCH). The order set auto-checks "Do Not Give Platelets for patients taking aspirin who are not undergoing a surgical procedure."

🔪 Going to neurosurgery

Platelet transfusion may be considered in conjunction with the operative plan — coordinate with neurosurgery.
General Hemostatic Treatments
🩹 rFVIIa & TXA
  • Recombinant factor VIIa (with or without the spot sign) — effect on functional outcome is unclear. COR 2b
  • Tranexamic acid (TXA) (with or without spot/black-hole/blend sign) — benefit is not well established. COR 2b
🧪 FASTEST Trial (rFVIIa)
Randomized rFVIIa vs placebo for acute ICH, ages 18–80, treated ≤120 min (with spot sign) or ≤90 min (without) of last-known-well; baseline non-contrast CT + CTA only. Kaiser Fontana is an enrolling SCAL site — consider trial eligibility on qualifying patients.
Diagnosis & Assessment
🧪 Initial Labs
Serum: CBC · BUN/Cr · LFTs · glucose · ESR and/or CRP · aPTT and PT/INR · specific tests for DOACs.

Urine: tox screen · pregnancy test.
🩻 Vascular Imaging Strategy
Look for an underlying macrovascular cause in higher-yield patients:
  • Lobar ICH <70 yrs, or deep/posterior-fossa ICH <45 yrs, or 45–70 yrs with no HTN → CTA/CTV recommended 1; MRI+MRA or cerebral angiogram reasonable 2a.
  • Spontaneous IVH with no parenchymal hemorrhage (any age), or CTA/MRA suggestive of a macrovascular etiology → cerebral angiogram recommended. 1
Seizure & AED Prophylaxis
⚡ Do ICH patients need AED prophylaxis?

No seizure

Avoid AEDs — do not routinely load levetiracetam (Keppra). 3: No benefit

Seizure / concern for seizure

Confirmed clinical or electrographic seizure → administer an AED. 1

Unexplained/fluctuating mental status or suspicion of seizure → cEEG (≥24 h) to detect electrographic seizures. 2a
ICP & Cerebral Edema
🧠 ICP Monitoring & Hyperosmolar Therapy
  • Ventricular drainage (EVD) should be performed to reduce mortality. COR 1
  • ICP monitoring and treatment might be considered. 2b
  • Bolus hyperosmolar therapy may be considered to transiently reduce ICP. 2b Early prophylactic hyperosmolar therapy is not well established. 2b
  • Do not give corticosteroids for elevated ICP 3: No benefit — OK only for hemorrhage related to a mass/tumor.
Hypertonic saline (3% NaCl) order set:
  • Bolus: 3% NaCl 250 mL IV over 15 min ×1 — may run via a peripheral IV.
  • Continuous: 3% NaCl 30–100 mL/hr (per local center agreement) ×72 h — via two separate peripheral IVs, or a central line / PICC.
  • Mannitol is an alternative hyperosmolar agent.
General ED Care, Nursing, Glucose & Temp
🏥 Disposition & Nursing
  • Care in a specialized inpatient unit with a multidisciplinary team (incl. virtual telestroke/neurosurgery). 1
  • If unavailable → transfer to a center with full high-acuity care. 1
  • Clinical hydrocephalus → transfer to a neurosurgical-capable center (EVD). 1
  • Use standardized order sets. 1
  • Formal dysphagia screen before any PO intake. 1
  • Continuous cardiac monitoring 24–72 h. 2a
  • Frequent neuro checks incl. GCS in the hyperacute phase. 1
🌡️ Glucose & Temperature
  • Monitor serum glucose (avoid hyper- and hypoglycemia). 1
  • Treat glucose <40–60 mg/dL. 1
  • Treat moderate–severe hyperglycemia (>180–200 mg/dL). 2a
  • Pharmacologically treat elevated temperature. 2b
  • Therapeutic hypothermia (<35 °C) — usefulness unclear. 2b
