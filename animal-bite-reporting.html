Animal Bite / Rabies Reporting — San Bernardino County

Mandatory clinician reporting of animal bites (17 CCR §2606) · who to call & official resources

About
17 CCR §2606

California has been declared a rabies area in all 58 counties, so under Title 17 CCR §2606 a clinician who treats any bite from a mammal (a species subject to rabies) must report it to the local health officer immediately — regardless of whether the animal appears ill, is vaccinated, or is later found healthy. Reporting triggers the standard 10-day observation / quarantine of the biting animal and the local rabies risk assessment.

📍 Jurisdiction matters: in San Bernardino County, bite reports route to the animal control agency responsible for where the bite occurred. SBC Animal Care covers the unincorporated areas and most contract cities, but some incorporated cities run their own animal control — route to that agency when applicable.

⚠️ Contact details below are compiled from public sources and should be verified against current SBC Animal Care / Public Health guidance and your KP SBC ED policy before relying on them — phone/fax numbers and forms change. The fax number in particular is from a secondary source; confirm before faxing PHI.

🏙️ Incorporated cities
Several cities operate their own animal control rather than SBC Animal Care. If the bite occurred in such a city, report to that city's animal control / police non-emergency line. Confirm the responsible agency for the bite location.
After-hours / public-health rabies questions
For human rabies post-exposure questions, the SBC Public Health Communicable Disease Section can be reached at (800) 722-4794 (after hours, Public Health Duty Officer (800) 472-2376).
🩺 ED Reminders

Reporting is one piece — also address the clinical bite workup per current guidance:

  • Rabies risk assessment / PEP: evaluate species, provocation, vaccination/observation status, and exposure type; start rabies PEP (RIG + vaccine series) when indicated per CDPH/ACIP. Bats and most wild carnivores are high-risk.
  • Wound care: copious irrigation; consider leaving high-risk wounds open; document neurovascular and tendon exam.
  • Tetanus prophylaxis per status.
  • Antibiotics: consider prophylaxis for high-risk bites (e.g., cat/human bites, hand/face, puncture, immunocompromised) per local guidance.
  • Animal observation: a healthy dog/cat/ferret can typically be observed 10 days; report so the local health officer can coordinate quarantine.

⚠️ Decision-support only — follow CDPH/ACIP rabies guidance, KP SBC protocols, and pharmacy/ID consultation. This page does not replace clinical judgment.
