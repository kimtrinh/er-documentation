🔁 General Protocol & Urgent Referrals
  • Urgent follow-up only: Secure chat the on-call physician and place an urgent referral. Please do not call at night. The on-call MD will coordinate follow-up with the patient the next day.
  • Infections / admissions: Internal medicine admits infections that are not drainable abscesses. Call IM if a patient needs admission with no drainable fluid collection — they can consult HNS if needed.
01🩸 Nosebleeds (Epistaxis)
  • First-line treatment: if no contraindications, give 1 gm IV tranexamic acid (TXA) + nebulized TXA (often helpful).
  • Controlled with packing & needs follow-up:
    • Place an outpatient referral.
    • Prescribe antibiotics.
    • Patient will be seen in 3–7 days depending on anticoagulation status.
  • Uncontrolled bleeding after packing: call HNS for help.

Bleeding may take some time to stop after packing placement.

04🦠 Peritonsillar Abscess (PTA)

PTA is a clinical diagnosis. Imaging is reserved for cases where the diagnosis is uncertain, or extension beyond the peritonsillar space is suspected.

ED treatment
  • Unasyn OR Clindamycin IV ×1
  • Ceftriaxone IM or IV ×1
  • Dexamethasone IV ×1
Discharge management
  • Clindamycin or Augmentin for 2 weeks. Follow up with PCP.
Escalation / HNS referral
  • Failed outpatient treatment: call HNS.
  • Recurrent PTA: outpatient referral to HNS to discuss tonsillectomy.
⚠️ Two PTA recipes exist on this site. The Peritonsillar Abscess Protocol under Hospital Protocols lists Decadron 10 mg IV + Rocephin 1–2 g IV, discharging on Augmentin or Clindamycin. Those are the same two drugs as above (Rocephin is ceftriaxone, Decadron is dexamethasone), but this Head & Neck guideline additionally specifies an IV Unasyn or Clindamycin dose and a 2-week discharge course. Confirm which your department is following.
05👂 Ear Pain & Infections
  • Facial nerve involvement: if the facial nerve is not functioning, call HNS.
  • Mastoiditis on imaging, without clinical symptoms:
    • No edema or erythema behind the ear → no call needed
    • Place an outpatient referral.
06🔇 Hearing Loss
  • Cerumen / infection: no call needed — place an outpatient referral.
  • Sudden hearing loss with a clear ear: no call needed — follow this workflow:
    • Prednisone 60 mg daily × 2 weeks
    • Urgent audiology referral.
    • Audiology will evaluate within 1–3 days. If true sudden loss is confirmed, the on-call MD will see the patient the same day.
