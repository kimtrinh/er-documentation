Alcohol MAT — ED AUD Pharmacotherapy

Medication for alcohol use disorder (AUD) · naltrexone · acamprosate · gabapentin · SBIRT · referral

Why This Matters
Start in the ED

Most EDs never start treatment for alcohol use disorder despite millions of alcohol-related visits. Yet AUD medications are safe, effective, and easy to begin in the ED, and same-visit initiation plus warm referral increases follow-up. A brief SBIRT (Screening, Brief Intervention, Referral to Treatment) takes minutes and reduces drinking and repeat visits.

⚠️ Decision-support summary — verify doses, contraindications, and interactions against current references and your KP SBC ED / Addiction Medicine protocol, and confirm with pharmacy. Not a substitute for clinical judgment.

Withdrawal ≠ AUD Pharmacotherapy
⚠️ Treat alcohol withdrawal first. Maintenance AUD medications below are not withdrawal management. Assess withdrawal (e.g., CIWA-Ar) and treat moderate–severe withdrawal with benzodiazepines/phenobarbital per protocol before or alongside starting maintenance pharmacotherapy.
💊 AUD Medications
MedicationTypical doseNotes / cautions
Naltrexone (PO)
first-line		50 mg PO daily (some start 25 mg)Can start while still drinking. Contraindicated with current opioid use/dependence (need ~7–10 opioid-free days) and in acute hepatitis / liver failure; caution in active liver disease — check LFTs.
Naltrexone (IM XR)380 mg IM every 4 weeksImproves adherence; same opioid/hepatic cautions as oral.
Acamprosate666 mg PO three times dailyNo hepatic metabolism → good in liver disease and when opioids are needed. Renally cleared: reduce to 333 mg TID if moderate renal impairment; avoid in severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30).
Gabapentin
second-line / off-label		Per protocol (titrated)May reduce cravings/withdrawal symptoms; consider misuse/dependence potential and renal dosing.
Topiramate / DisulfiramSpecialist / selected patientsTopiramate off-label (titrate, cognitive/teratogenic cautions). Disulfiram only with abstinence commitment + supervision.

Pick by comorbidity: liver disease or on opioids → acamprosate; otherwise naltrexone (esp. if reducing heavy drinking is the goal). Start in the ED and refer for ongoing management.

💬 How to Talk to the Patient

Use SBIRT: screen, give brief feedback, and refer — non-judgmentally, with permission.

"Would it be okay if we talked about your drinking for a minute?"
"There are medications that reduce cravings and help people cut back or stop — and we can start one today if you're open to it."
"This is common and treatable, and I'm not here to judge. What, if anything, worries you about your drinking?"
  • Ask permission; offer clear, nonjudgmental feedback linking the visit to alcohol when relevant.
  • Use person-first language ("person with alcohol use disorder").
  • Offer a concrete next step (medication today + a referral) rather than only advice to "cut down."
